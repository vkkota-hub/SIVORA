# SIVORA website reference research

Research date: 23 September 2026. The design should borrow patterns, not visual assets or copy.

## Reference patterns

### Atos — https://atos.net/en/
- Large enterprise information architecture: Services, Industries, Insights, About, Careers.
- Deep service taxonomy is handled through a mega-menu rather than crowding the homepage.
- Homepage balances company positioning, current news, thought leadership, client success and careers.
- Strong pattern for SIVORA: keep the homepage concise, but let Services and Industries pages carry detail.

### Red Hat — https://www.redhat.com/en/services/consulting
- Consulting page starts with a direct outcome-led proposition, then groups services into a small number of clear areas.
- Explains “why”, then “how we work”, then supporting resources and a contact CTA.
- Strong pattern for SIVORA: practical service descriptions and a visible delivery approach, not abstract transformation language.

### CGI — https://www.cgi.com/en/ and https://www.cgi.com/en/careers
- Strong separation between service/company content and careers content.
- Careers uses simple, memorable themes and role categories rather than overloading users with copy.
- Repeats a consistent corporate positioning line in the footer.
- Strong pattern for SIVORA: distinct Careers page, clear role list and a compact admin-managed jobs area.

### Northvolt — https://northvolt.com/
- Very clean navigation and strong visual storytelling.
- Homepage mixes mission, partner credibility, solution categories, careers, sustainability and news.
- Footer carries a real address, contact details and deep links.
- Strong pattern for SIVORA: confident use of whitespace, image/visual moments and a useful footer rather than a decorative one.

### eBay corporate — https://www.ebayinc.com/
- Story-led corporate homepage with purpose, ecosystem facts, news and human/business impact.
- The copy is conversational and concrete rather than full of consulting jargon.
- Strong pattern for SIVORA: use real, specific language and avoid generic “unlock, empower, transform” repetition.

### Standard Chartered careers — https://www.sc.com/en/global-careers
- Strong hero statement followed by purpose and career pathways.
- Uses clear segmentation instead of one long careers page.
- Strong pattern for SIVORA: direct value proposition for candidates and concise paths to open roles.

### BNP Paribas — https://group.bnpparibas/en/ and https://group.bnpparibas/en/careers
- Corporate site gives careers a prominent place alongside company news, commitments and group information.
- Careers provides job search, candidate profile segmentation and profession categories.
- Strong pattern for SIVORA: allow jobs to be filtered later if the role count grows, but keep the MVP list simple.

### Bank of America careers — https://careers.bankofamerica.com/en-us
- Guided discovery helps users find relevant career areas.
- Strong pattern for SIVORA: when the company has many roles, add filters or a guided role finder. Not needed for the first few jobs.

### HSBC — https://www.hsbc.com/
- Editorial, news-led corporate experience with prominent current topics, security/accessibility links and clear footer structure.
- Strong pattern for SIVORA: use Insights only when there is genuinely useful, maintained content; make privacy/security/accessibility visible.

### Barclays — https://www.ib.barclays/
- Accessible public Barclays pages reviewed show a concise solutions-led presentation with strong visual hierarchy.
- The main home.barclays homepage could not be fully crawled during this research, so no framework-specific assumptions are made.
- Strong pattern for SIVORA: short value propositions and clear business language.

## Shared enterprise patterns worth using
1. A small primary navigation with deeper detail behind Services/Industries.
2. A strong, plain-language first screen with one primary CTA.
3. Proof or credibility content close to the top — but only when the proof is real.
4. Separate service, industry, careers and insight information architecture.
5. Human-readable service copy focused on business problems and operating outcomes.
6. Accessible typography, restrained motion, large whitespace and clear hierarchy.
7. A useful footer with office/contact details, privacy/accessibility links and social channels.
8. Careers should be manageable without code once hiring activity starts.

## What SIVORA should not copy
- No fake client logos, awards, case studies or metrics.
- No oversized mega-menu until the service catalogue actually needs one.
- No heavy news feed before there is a real editorial cadence.
- No generic consulting paragraphs written only for keyword density.
- No glossy 3D branding, excessive gradients, floating glass cards or template-style feature boxes.
